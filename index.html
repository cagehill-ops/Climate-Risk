Methods, assumptions & sources +

The arithmetic

Headroom = available supply − demand. Shortfall is the positive magnitude of negative headroom. The crossing marks equality within the displayed heat range. With H = extra heat, q = reduction ÷ 100 and C = extra capacity, the crossing is H* = [S₀ + C − D₀(1 − q)] ÷ [β + α(1 − q)]. A crossing outside 0–4°C is not extrapolated on this site. Curves use the same reduction and added capacity at every heat level.

Added capacity is net power dependable at the modeled peak; the model does not determine construction, fuel, storage duration, or delivery. Demand reduction is incremental to the assessment’s adjusted demand and must occur at the coincident peak.

Reference and assumptions

The baseline arithmetic is D₀ = (82,739 − 432 + 1,105) ÷ 1,000 = 83.412 GW, including rooftop-PV and large-flexible-load adjustments. S₀ = (96,988 − 5,034) ÷ 1,000 = 91.954 GW after typical outages. One GW equals 1,000 MW; GW measures power, while GWh measures energy over time. The reference already reflects summer-rated capability, renewable and storage contributions, and typical outages. Only the assumed additional heat loss is applied above that reference; baseline supply is not derated again. The response rates are illustrative assumptions, not published ERCOT estimates. Slider bounds, steps, presets and the starting point are teaching choices; the initial heat is chosen near the boundary, not as likely weather.

A worked example

At +4°C with a 5% demand reduction and 2 GW added capacity, heat-adjusted demand before reduction is 91.412 GW. Reducing it by 4.5706 GW gives 86.8414 GW demand; modeled supply is 91.954 GW, leaving 5.1126 GW headroom. Without adaptations, the same heat produces −1.458 GW headroom. These are calculations under the reference assumptions, not predictions.

Limits

Heat is a hazard; demand and grid resources are exposed to it, while their vulnerabilities determine consequences. Adaptations can change that exposure or vulnerability. Texas has warmed and extreme heat is changing, but this local peak offset cannot be mapped to a particular year or global warming level. Zero added heat still means summer peak conditions, not zero hazard or zero grid risk. Population, economic activity and other demand drivers are held fixed. The model omits operating reserve requirements, contingencies, transmission constraints, generator-failure distributions, local distribution outages, chronology, humidity, storage duration and costs. Winter storms and hurricanes also matter to Texas, but this experiment focuses on summer heat. It is not calibrated to predict outages or reproduce ERCOT reliability analysis.
