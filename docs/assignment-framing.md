# Decision-maker, decision, and course concept

Our project, **Texas Grid Under Heat**, is designed for an ERCOT resource planner deciding how much peak-demand reduction and additional dependable capacity could help the grid meet electricity demand during extreme summer heat. The interactive website holds other drivers of demand constant and lets users test how additional local heat, peak-demand reduction, and capacity available at the modeled peak jointly change the balance between electricity demand and supply. It illustrates climate risk through the relationship between a physical hazard (heat), exposure (electricity demand in ERCOT), vulnerability (temperature-sensitive demand and limited available supply), and adaptation (reducing demand and adding dependable capacity). The main lesson is threshold behavior: small changes can move demand above available supply, while adaptation can shift that crossing point. This is a conditional educational stress test, not a forecast of blackout probability.
